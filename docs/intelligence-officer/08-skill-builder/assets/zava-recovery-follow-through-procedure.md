# Zava Recovery Follow-through Procedure

## Purpose

Help a service lead determine what should happen next for a warranty or goodwill request and prepare an accurate customer update. Keep approval, fulfillment, and customer communication distinct: a request for recovery is not an approval, and an approval is not evidence that recovery has been delivered.

## 1. Identify the request

Use the request code to identify the correct warranty or goodwill request. If the code is missing, cannot be found, or identifies more than one request, obtain clarification before proceeding.

Confirm the customer, related service case, product, requested recovery, and requested amount. Review the business justification, customer impact, warranty status, and submitted date. Treat missing information as not recorded rather than assuming a value.

## 2. Review the approval and fulfillment records

Review the request status, assigned exception policy, required approval level, routing notes, all related approval decisions, and fulfillment notes.

Read the approval history in decision-date order, including approval levels, approvers, approved amounts, comments, and any follow-up requirements. A Pending decision with no approver or decision date means the request is awaiting review, not that it was declined.

Distinguish historical blockers from current ones. An earlier Needs More Information decision does not remain the current approval outcome when a later recorded approval supersedes it. Review any conditions or follow-up requirements that remain in the later decision.

Do not use record codes or an assumed sequence to resolve missing or tied decision dates. If the current outcome cannot be established, or the request status and decision history materially conflict, ask the service lead to clarify the records before making a fulfillment commitment.

## 3. Determine the next action

| Current position | Follow-through action |
| --- | --- |
| Draft or missing submission information | Identify what must be completed before submission. Do not describe the request as already under approval review. |
| No assigned exception policy or required approval level | Identify the routing information that is missing and refer the request for routing review. Do not select a policy or approval level on the team's behalf. |
| Needs Manual Review | Explain the recorded routing issue and refer it to the service lead for resolution. Do not bypass the review requirement. |
| Awaiting approval | Identify the required approval level and the outstanding review. Do not promise the requested replacement, refund, or credit. |
| Current decision is Needs More Information | List the specific information requested in the decision comments. Arrange for it to be collected and returned for review. If the comments do not explain what is needed, seek clarification. |
| Approved, with fulfillment not recorded as complete | Review the approved recovery, approved amount, approval level, and outstanding conditions before recommending a fulfillment review. |
| Request status is Rejected | Prepare an explanation using the recorded reason. If the reason is missing or conflicts with the approval history, seek clarification rather than inventing an explanation. |
| Request status is Fulfilled | Review the fulfillment notes and prepare a completion update supported by those records. Do not recommend issuing the recovery again. |

Apply the action that addresses the current blocker first. Missing routing, unresolved information requests, or conflicting records must not be treated as authorization to fulfill a request.

## 4. Review approved requests for fulfillment

Confirm that the recorded approval matches the required approval level and establishes what was authorized. Keep the requested amount separate from the approved amount; if the approved amount is missing, do not substitute the requested amount.

Review approval comments and follow-up requirements for unresolved conditions. Refer a request with sufficient approval information to the fulfillment specialist for review. If authorization or required information is incomplete, identify the gap before recommending that handoff.

Use Request Status and Fulfillment Notes to establish what has been completed. An Approved decision alone does not establish that a replacement has shipped, a refund has been processed, or a credit has been applied. Missing fulfillment notes do not prove either completion or non-completion.

## 5. Prepare the customer-response draft

Draft a short, customer-facing update that acknowledges the issue, explains the supported current position, and describes the next step.

For requests awaiting review, explain that review is pending without implying approval. For information requests, distinguish information the customer must provide from information the service team should confirm internally. For approved requests, distinguish approval from fulfillment. For fulfilled requests, describe only the completion details supported by the records.

Do not invent delivery dates, refund timing, amounts, approvals, or guarantees. Do not include internal policy identifiers, internal deliberations, or unnecessary personal information.

## 6. Present the follow-through recommendation

Provide the service lead with the current position, the records supporting it, the next recommended action, any unresolved information or conditions, and the customer-response draft.

This procedure ends with a recommendation and a draft. Approval decisions, record changes, fulfillment, and sending the customer response remain separate actions performed by authorized staff.
